MiniMe · a language model trained from scratch

How much of a person’s voice can a tiny model buy?

MiniMe is a -parameter language model trained from scratch to write in one person’s voice, on free GPU-hours. Beside it, a controlled study at 17M parameters measures what that voice costs in general language ability.

Samples

What the models wrote, drawn at random rather than picked. At the owner’s request a script chose them without anyone reading them: from the test split, it kept the samples that pass the gate below and a word filter for profanity, slurs and insults (English and Vietnamese), with prose in English and of moderate length, then drew three voice prompts, one general text and one function with a fixed seed. Each prompt appears twice: the final model’s sample, then the unadapted flagship’s. A word filter is not a reading, so a sample can still say something odd. A voice sample is the model’s next message in a held-out conversation, written after the owner’s preceding messages. Those prompts are the owner’s real messages and are not shown.

Each sample passed a gate before it was published: no run of more than tokens shared with the training chat, and nothing the corpus’s scrubber finds (names, handles, phone numbers, emails, links).

The samples are not published yet.

Tokens such as <NAME> are the corpus’s redactions, which the model learned to write.

What stays private

The owner’s messages, the tokenizer trained on them, the list of names redacted from them and every model checkpoint stay off the internet. MiniMe runs only on the owner’s laptop. There is no endpoint, public or private, and no live model behind this page.

Memorisation: checked, not ruled out

A small model can reproduce its training text verbatim, other people’s words included. The memorisation check gave each model 700 of the owner’s training messages and 700 held-out ones, each after the messages before it, and counted exact reproductions. It could detect memorisation only if the run that overfit the chat (E1 100/0, about seven passes) separated from a model that never saw it (P1). It did not, so by its own rule the check is blind at this scale and no claim of low memorisation is made from it.

A run shared with the training chat is the longest token sequence a voice sample has in common with the owner’s training messages. The owner’s own held-out messages share as much with their past, so overlap alone cannot tell recall from style.

Limits

  • At this size a model learns voice, not facts: words, rhythm, punctuation, register. It knows nothing reliable about the owner’s life and does not hold a conversation.
  • One person’s messages: every finding about the persona is a case study of one.
  • The flagship is a single run. Its distance from the probes mixes size, budget and context, so none of it is attributed to any one of them.
  • The personal corpus is the owner’s English and code-switched Instagram messages; mainly-Vietnamese messages were left out by design.
